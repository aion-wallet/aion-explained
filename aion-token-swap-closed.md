The official Aion token swap closed on 31 January 2022, so an ERC-20 AION balance on Ethereum can no longer be converted to native AION through the project, and no later process has reopened it.

For most holders this changes nothing: the native coin is the one the wallet, the exchanges and the staking system use. For a holder who has been waiting to convert an old balance, it is a definitive answer rather than a delay.

## You'll see this if

- You have an AION token entry in an Ethereum wallet.
- An old guide describes a swap form that no longer exists.
- Someone has offered to convert your ERC-20 balance.
- You are trying to work out whether your holdings are native or not.

## What the swap did

It moved holders from the ERC-20 token issued during the sale onto the network's own coin. Tokens were locked on Ethereum and native AION was issued on mainnet, with a manual route for late conversions.

The initial phase completed in December 2018, and the manual route continued for holders who had missed it. Both are now closed.

## Why it ended

A conversion bridge is an ongoing operational commitment: it holds value on one chain and issues it on another, and it has to be maintained for as long as the route exists.

Closing a bridge is a deliberate decision with a deadline, which is why the date matters more than the mechanism. A page that still describes the process is describing something that has ended, not something that is slow.

## What remains true

The native coin is unaffected. It trades, it stakes, it pays fees, and it is held in wallets that speak the Aion address format.

The ERC-20 token still exists as a contract balance on Ethereum. It can be viewed, and in principle transferred as a token, but it is not native AION and cannot be swapped through the project.

Nothing about the closure affects a wallet that already holds native coins, which is why most holders never encounter it.

## What to do with an old balance

Check it on an Ethereum explorer, record what it is, and treat it as historical. Do not send it to anyone who offers to convert it.

A conversion offer made now is either a different asset entirely or a straightforward theft. The project has published a deadline, and the deadline has passed.

If you also hold native AION and are not sure which is which, the [Aion ERC-20 or mainnet](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-explained/blob/main/aion-erc-20-or-mainnet.md) article compares the address formats, which is the quickest test.

## Telling native from ERC-20

Open the wallet's receive screen. A native Aion address is 66 characters and begins `0xa0`; an Ethereum address is 42 characters.

If the wallet has no Aion network configured at all, whatever it shows for AION is the ERC-20 token. The [Aion address format](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-wallet-help/blob/main/aion-address-format-0xa0.md) guide explains the structure behind the length.

If you are asked to prove ownership of an old ERC-20 balance, remember that proof is a signature, never a phrase. A request for words or for a keystore file is not a verification step, whatever the page calls it.

The closure sits inside a longer sequence of changes, and the [what happened to Aion](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-explained/blob/main/what-happened-to-aion.md) article places it alongside the rebrand and the ecosystem contraction that are often mistaken for it.

## Prevention

Do not act on a swap page you find by searching. The legitimate one is closed, so anything offering the service is not it.

Keep a record of which network each balance sits on. A note that says "AION, mainnet" or "AION, ERC-20" is enough to prevent a mistaken send.

If you acquire AION today, take it on mainnet and hold it in a wallet that names the network, so the question does not arise later.

## The bridge in context

The swap was one of the earliest production cross-chain bridges, and its closure is a useful case study in what a bridge actually is: a service with an operator, a reserve and a lifetime.

When the service ends, the asset on the far side does not disappear, and the asset on the near side is not recalled. What ends is the conversion route between them. That is why a closed swap leaves two populations of holders rather than one broken network.

The project's interoperability thesis outlived the specific bridge, and the [Aion vs the OAN](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-explained/blob/main/aion-vs-the-oan.md) article describes how the positioning changed around the same period without a technical migration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there any way to convert ERC-20 AION now?

Not through the project. The official swap closed on 31 January 2022.

### Was the deadline extended?

The manual route was retired and the published deadline passed. No later conversion process is part of the project.

### Can I send ERC-20 AION to an Aion address?

No. They are different networks and different address formats, and the transfer would not arrive.

### Does the closure affect native AION holders?

No. Native coins, staking positions and wallet balances were unaffected.

Aion is not a Chinese company: it was founded and built in Toronto, Canada, by a Canadian team, and the question usually comes from a name collision or from a listing page that grouped it with unrelated projects.

The origin is checkable. The confusion has specific causes, and naming them is more useful than a flat denial.

## You'll see this if

- A forum post or a video described Aion as a Chinese project.
- A listing page grouped it with assets from a different region.
- You are checking a project's jurisdiction before using it.
- You are comparing it with a chain that genuinely has that origin.

## Where the project was built

Toronto. The founding company, Nuco, was based there, the founders worked from there, and the project's early public material is Canadian.

The foundation structure that later stewarded the network was established offshore, in the Cayman Islands, which is a common arrangement for token projects and not evidence of a different engineering origin. The [who founded Aion](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-explained/blob/main/who-founded-aion.md) article names the people and the entities.

## Where the confusion comes from

The name. "Aion" is a short, widely used word that appears in game titles, company names and product names in several languages. A search that does not include the word blockchain returns almost anything.

Exchange listings. Aggregators group assets by market and by tag, and a tag applied to one asset can be read as a description of another. A listing page is not a statement of a project's origin.

Older coverage. Some early articles about interoperability projects described Asian partnerships and read, at a glance, like statements about where the team was. A partnership is not an origin.

## What can be verified

Founders, company and city are documented in interviews and in the project's own material. The foundation reports published during the OAN period describe the governance structure.

What cannot be verified from public sources is a current corporate registration for the entity behind the network. That is a gap in the record, not evidence of a hidden origin, and anyone needing it for a legal purpose should obtain it directly.

## Why origin matters, and where it does not

Jurisdiction matters for legal and tax questions, and for understanding which laws a foundation operates under.

It does not change how the software works. The wallet is non-custodial, the keys are yours, and the chain is identified by chain ID 256 regardless of who founded it.

It also does not change the risk model. A wallet in Toronto and a wallet in any other city fail in the same ways, which is why the [Aion wallet support](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-wallet-help/blob/main/aion-wallet-support.md) guide is about credentials rather than about geography.

## What to check instead

If the real question is whether a project is legitimate, origin is a weak proxy. Check the release history, the repository activity and whether the installers are published with checksums, as described in the [Aion wallet download verify](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-wallet-help/blob/main/aion-wallet-download-verify.md) guide.

If the question is regulatory, ask a professional in your jurisdiction rather than a guide.

One practical test is language. The project's early documentation, interviews and hiring were in English and centred on North American conferences, which is consistent with the Toronto origin and inconsistent with the claim that it was built elsewhere under another name.

## Prevention

Use the full name in searches: Aion network, Aion blockchain, or AION token. The bare word will not resolve to this project.

Treat listing tags and category labels as aggregator metadata, not as facts about a team.

Date claims about a project's structure, since foundations and companies change over a decade.

## Jurisdiction, foundation and the honest gap

A foundation registered in the Cayman Islands is a governance vehicle. It holds assets, makes grants and signs agreements, and it does so under the laws of the place it is registered in. That is a different question from where the code is written or where the founders live.

Aion's founders worked from Toronto, and the early material about the project is Canadian. The foundation structure came later and exists to steward the network rather than to relocate it.

What is missing from the public record is a current registration extract for whichever entity operates today. That gap is worth stating plainly instead of filling with a guess: the [who founded Aion](https://github.com/aion-wallet/aion-explained/blob/main/who-founded-aion.md) article lists what is documented and what is not, and a reader who needs the legal detail should request it from the entity rather than infer it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Aion Chinese?

No. It was founded and built in Toronto, Canada.

### Why do some sources say otherwise?

Name collisions, aggregator tags and early coverage of Asian partnerships are the usual causes.

### Where is the foundation registered?

The Aion Foundation was established in the Cayman Islands in 2018, and governance later sat with The Open Foundation. Current registration is not published in a form this guide can verify.

### Does the origin affect the wallet?

No. The software behaves the same way regardless of where its authors are based.
